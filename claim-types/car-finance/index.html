Car finance affordability claims

The short answer

If your car finance lender didn’t properly check you could afford the monthly payments, you could complain. If it’s upheld, you could get back some of what you paid and have negative entries removed from your credit file. This is separate from car finance commission claims.

What counts as unaffordable car finance lending

Car finance is credit, so the finance company had to check you could afford the monthly payments for the whole agreement, not just that you were likely to pay. A car deal often means hundreds of pounds a month over three or four years, so the checks should match that.

Affordability claims tend to involve used cars bought on hire purchase or conditional sale from specialist lenders, but any car finance agreement can be looked at.

  • Apr 2014Under the FCA’s rules, car finance lenders must check you can afford the agreement, with more thorough checks for bigger payments and longer terms.
  • Nov 2018More detailed rules: the lender must assess the risk to your finances, not just its own risk, looking at income and essential outgoings.
  • OmbudsmanIf the checks weren’t proportionate, the ombudsman asks what proper checks would have shown. Relying on average living costs instead of your real spending isn’t always enough.

Signs you could have a claim

  1. 01The payments left you short every monthIf the car payment pushed you into your overdraft or onto other credit, it wasn’t affordable.
  2. 02Nobody asked about your outgoingsA lender that only looked at your credit score or used averages may not have checked properly.
  3. 03You fell behind soon afterMissed payments early in the agreement can show it was never sustainable.
  4. 04You had other debts or defaultsProblems on your credit file when you applied were visible to the lender.
  5. 05The car was handed back or takenEnding an agreement early because you couldn’t keep up doesn’t stop you complaining about the lending decision.

Because you had use of the car, the lender can usually keep a fair amount for that use. In one upheld decision this was set at £230 a month. Anything you paid above that, including your deposit, comes back to you with interest.

If you still have the car, the lender may be told to end the agreement and take the car back. If what you’ve paid is less than the fair usage amount, the difference goes on an affordable repayment plan.

Ombudsman decisions on car finance complaints

Published Financial Ombudsman decisions show how these complaints are judged. Every complaint is decided on its own facts, so these are examples, not a guide to what you’ll get.

  • UpheldAdvantage Finance

    Advantage never asked about the customer’s spending, and his bank statements showed too little spare income for about £235 a month on a used-car hire purchase deal. He got back his deposit and payments, less £230 a month for the 27 months he had the car, with interest.

    Read decision DRN-3330407

  • Not upheldMoneybarn

    Moneybarn relied on average living costs instead of checking the customer’s real spending, so its checks didn’t go far enough. Her bank statements still showed she could afford the payments, so nothing was due.

    Read decision DRN-4159548

Affordability claims and commission claims

You may have heard about car finance commission claims. Those are about hidden or high commission paid to the dealer, and the FCA has set up a redress scheme for them covering agreements from April 2007.

An affordability claim is different. It’s about whether the lender should have lent to you at all. You can have one, the other, or both on the same agreement.

Car finance claim questions

Is this the same as the car finance commission claims?

No. Commission claims are about payments to the dealer. Affordability claims are about whether the lender checked you could afford the payments.

Can I claim if I still have the car?

Yes. If the complaint is upheld, the lender may end the agreement and take the car back, and refund anything you paid above a fair amount for using it.

Can I claim if the car was repossessed or I handed it back?

Yes. You can still complain about whether the lending was affordable in the first place.

Does PCP count?

Yes. Hire purchase, PCP and conditional sale are all covered by the affordability rules.

How far back can I claim?

Usually six years from the agreement, or three years from when you realised you had cause to complain, whichever is later.

How to complain about car finance yourself

You can complain to the finance company directly and for free. That’s the company named on your agreement, not the dealer. Say you want its affordability checks reviewed.

It has eight weeks to reply. If you’re unhappy with the answer, or it hasn’t replied in time, you can take it to the Financial Ombudsman, normally within six months of the final response. That’s free too.
