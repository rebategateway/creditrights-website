Credit and store card refund claims

The short answer

If a card provider gave you a card, or kept raising your limit, when you couldn’t afford it, you could get back the interest and charges on that lending. Each limit increase was a separate decision that should have been checked.

What counts as unaffordable credit card lending

A credit card is a running account: you can keep borrowing up to the limit. That makes the limit the thing that matters. The provider had to check the opening limit was affordable, and check again before any significant increase.

Many card claims are about limits that climbed while the balance never came down. A card that started at £500 and reached several thousand pounds, while only minimum payments were being made, is a common pattern in upheld complaints.

  • Apr 2014Under the FCA’s rules, card providers must assess whether you can afford the credit before opening an account or significantly raising the limit.
  • Mar 2018Persistent debt rules: after 18 months of paying more in interest and charges than off the balance, the provider must contact you. About nine months later it must remind you. At 36 months it must offer a way to repay over a reasonable time, usually three to four years, and if you can’t, it must show forbearance, which can mean reducing or cancelling interest and charges.
  • Nov 2018More detailed rules on affordability checks, including looking at income and essential outgoings, not just credit history.

Signs you could have a claim

  1. 01Your limit went up while you were only paying the minimumMinimum payments month after month, then an increase, is one of the clearest signs the provider should have looked harder.
  2. 02You maxed out the card soon after getting itUsing the whole limit straight away can show you needed the money to get by, not that you could afford to borrow it.
  3. 03You used it for bills, essentials or cashCash withdrawals and essential spending on a card are often signs of financial difficulty.
  4. 04You had other debts building upOther cards, loans or missed payments on your credit file were visible to the provider when it decided your limit.
  5. 05You were stuck in persistent debtIf you paid more in interest and charges than off the balance for 18 months, the provider had to contact you. Limit increases offered while you were in persistent debt are worth looking at.

If a complaint is upheld, the provider usually removes interest and charges on the part of the balance that shouldn’t have been lent, from the date of the unfair decision. For example, if an increase from £1,000 to £2,000 was unfair, interest on the balance above £1,000 comes off from that date.

If you still owe money, the refund reduces your balance first, and anything left over is paid to you with interest added.

Credit card claim questions

Can I claim for limit increases?

Yes. Each increase was a separate lending decision, so the provider should have checked it was affordable each time.

Can I claim for a store card?

Yes. Store cards are credit cards for one retailer, and the same lending rules apply.

What does persistent debt mean?

Paying more in interest, fees and charges than off what you borrowed over 18 months. Since 2018 card providers have had to contact people in that position, and at 36 months offer a way to clear the balance.

I only ever paid the minimum. Does that help?

It can. Long periods of minimum payments, especially before a limit increase, are something the ombudsman looks at.

How far back can I claim?

Usually six years from the lending, or three years from when you realised you had cause to complain, whichever is later.

How to complain about a credit card yourself

You can complain to the card provider directly and for free. Say which account, when the limit increases happened, and that you want them reviewed for affordability.

The provider has eight weeks to reply. If you’re unhappy with the answer, or it hasn’t replied in time, you can take it to the Financial Ombudsman, normally within six months of the final response. That’s free too.
