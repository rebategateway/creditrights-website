Can I claim if my debt was sold to a debt collector?

The short answer

Yes. If the original lending was unaffordable, you complain to the lender that made the decision to lend, not the debt collector. If the complaint is upheld, the lender usually buys the debt back, or works with the new owner to take off the interest and charges.

Who to complain to

Financial Ombudsman decisions draw a clear line. The lender that agreed the credit was responsible for checking you could afford it. The company that bought the debt, or collects it, is responsible only for how it has handled the account since.

In one decision about an account Lowell had taken over, the ombudsman said the lending assessment was the original lender’s responsibility, and Lowell didn’t have to check whether the account was irresponsibly lent when it bought it. A decision about a debt managed by Capquest said the same: the borrower could raise the lending with the original lender directly.

How to claim when your debt has been sold

Selling the debt doesn’t end the original lender’s responsibility for the lending. You can complain to the lender yourself, or use our check and we introduce your claim to a specialist who makes the complaint for you.

  1. 01Find the original lenderLook for its name on your credit file or the collector’s letters. Our bank and lender pages show the company behind each brand.
  2. 02Complain to the original lenderSay the borrowing was unaffordable, when it started, and that the debt has since been sold.
  3. 03Wait up to eight weeksThe lender has eight weeks to send its final response.
  4. 04Take it to the Financial OmbudsmanIf you disagree with the answer, or it’s late, the ombudsman can look at it. You normally have six months from the final response.

What happens to the debt if you win

In one decision about a Halifax credit card whose balance had been sold to a third party in 2022, the ombudsman upheld the complaint and said Halifax should buy the debt back. If it couldn’t, it had to work with the new owner to take the interest and charges off the account, refund anything paid above what was fairly owed, with interest, or agree an affordable way to repay the rest.

Once the corrected balance is repaid, adverse information about the lending is removed from your credit file.

Complaints about the debt collector itself

How a collector has chased the debt since it took over is a separate matter. That complaint goes to the collector, and the ombudsman looks at its own conduct only, not the original decision to lend.

Questions

Lowell bought my debt. Can I still complain about the lending?

Yes. Complain to the lender that made the decision to lend. Lowell, or any other company that bought the debt, isn’t responsible for that decision.

Do I complain to the debt collector or the original lender?

About whether you could afford the lending: the original lender. About how the debt has been collected since: the collector.

What happens to my debt if the complaint is upheld?

The lender usually buys the debt back, or works with the new owner to take off the interest and charges. Anything you paid above what you fairly owed is refunded with interest.

Can I claim if the debt is paid off or closed?

Yes. Paid-off and closed accounts can still be looked at, subject to the time limits: usually six years from the lending, or three years from when you realised you had cause to complain.
