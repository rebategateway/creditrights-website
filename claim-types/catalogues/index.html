Catalogue account refund claims

The short answer

If a catalogue or online shopping account kept raising your limit while you were only making minimum payments, you could get back the interest and charges on the lending that shouldn’t have happened.

What counts as unaffordable catalogue lending

A catalogue or shopping account lets you buy now and pay over time, with interest on the balance. It works like a credit card for one retailer or group of retailers, so the same lending rules apply.

The provider had to check the opening limit was affordable and check again before significant increases. Claims usually involve limits that crept up, sometimes with a “good news” letter, while the balance stayed close to the limit.

  • Apr 2014Under the FCA’s rules, catalogue credit providers must assess whether you can afford the credit before opening an account or significantly raising the limit.
  • Nov 2018More detailed rules on affordability checks, including looking at income and essential outgoings.
  • Dec 2018Catalogue and store card providers must not raise the credit limit for customers at risk of financial difficulty. From March 2019 customers had to be given control over limit increases.
  • Jun 2019Persistent debt rules extended to catalogue accounts: providers must step in after 18 months of paying more in interest and charges than off the balance.

Signs you could have a claim

  1. 01Your limit kept going upSeveral increases in a short time, especially unasked, are a common feature of upheld complaints.
  2. 02Your balance was always close to the limitA balance that never came down suggested you were relying on the account.
  3. 03You only ever made the minimum paymentYears of minimum payments mean you paid mostly interest.
  4. 04You used it for essentialsClothes for the children, bedding or appliances you couldn’t otherwise afford point to financial difficulty.
  5. 05You had other debts or missed paymentsProblems on your credit file were visible when the provider raised your limit.

If a complaint is upheld, the provider usually removes interest and charges on the part of the balance above the last fair limit, from the date of the unfair increase.

If you still owe money, the refund reduces the balance first. Anything left over is paid to you with interest added.

Catalogue claim questions

Is a catalogue account the same as a credit card?

For lending purposes, yes. It’s a running account with a limit and interest, so the same affordability rules apply.

I have accounts with several brands. Do I complain to each?

Check who provides the credit. Several brands can belong to one provider, and one complaint can cover all of them.

Can I claim if the account is closed?

Yes. Closed accounts can still be looked at, subject to the time limits.

What about buy now pay later on my catalogue account?

Buy now pay later offers on a catalogue account are part of a regulated credit account, so they’re covered. Since March 2019 providers have had to explain them clearly and remind you before the offer period ends.

How far back can I claim?

Usually six years from the lending, or three years from when you realised you had cause to complain, whichever is later.

How to complain about a catalogue account yourself

You can complain to the credit provider directly and for free. Say which account and when the limit increases happened, and ask for them to be reviewed for affordability.

The provider has eight weeks to reply. If you’re unhappy with the answer, or it hasn’t replied in time, you can take it to the Financial Ombudsman, normally within six months of the final response. That’s free too.
