Can I claim back catalogue interest?

The short answer

Yes, if the account should never have been allowed to reach the limit it did. Every limit increase was a lending decision, so if your limit kept going up while you were only making minimum payments or already struggling, the interest and charges on that lending can be refunded.

When you can claim back catalogue interest

A catalogue or online shopping account works like a credit card for one retailer or group of retailers, so the same lending rules apply. The provider had to check the opening limit was affordable, and check again before significant increases.

Claims usually involve limits that crept up while the balance stayed close to the limit. These are the patterns that most often lead to a refund:

  1. 01Your limit kept going upSeveral increases in a short time, especially ones you didn’t ask for.
  2. 02Your balance was always close to the limitA balance that never came down suggested you were relying on the account.
  3. 03You only ever made the minimum paymentYears of minimum payments mean you paid mostly interest.
  4. 04You had other debts or missed paymentsProblems on your credit file were visible when the provider raised your limit.

The rules catalogue providers have to follow

Since April 2014, under the FCA’s rules, catalogue credit providers have had to assess whether you can afford the credit before opening an account or significantly raising the limit. More detailed rules on affordability checks, including looking at income and essential outgoings, followed in November 2018.

From December 2018 catalogue and store card providers could not raise the limit for customers at risk of financial difficulty, and from June 2019 the persistent debt rules applied to catalogue accounts too: providers have to step in after 18 months of paying more in interest and charges than off the balance.

What the ombudsman has decided

Published Financial Ombudsman decisions show how catalogue complaints are judged. Each is decided on its own facts, so these are examples:

  1. 01Grattan (Look Again), upheldA £200 limit raised to £500 within four months in 2014. Grattan should have seen the customer was unlikely to repay sustainably, and all interest, fees and charges were to be removed.
  2. 02Studio, upheldA £200 limit opened in June 2019 and raised to £600 that November. Studio Retail Limited acted unfairly in extending credit and was told to refund all interest and charges.
  3. 03Next, upheldA NextPay limit raised from £400 to £3,000 in September 2017 without proportionate checks. Interest and charges were to be removed from that date.
  4. 04Freemans, upheldAn account opened in July 2018 with a £100 limit. The customer’s credit file showed arrears and borrowing near its limits, so the lending was unfair.

How to claim back catalogue interest

You can complain to the provider yourself, online or in writing, or use our check and we introduce your claim to a specialist who makes the complaint for you.

  1. 01Find out who provides the creditSeveral catalogue brands can belong to one provider, and one complaint can cover all of them.
  2. 02Complain to the providerSay which accounts, roughly when the limit went up, and ask for the lending to be reviewed for affordability.
  3. 03Wait up to eight weeksThe provider has eight weeks to send its final response.
  4. 04Take it to the Financial OmbudsmanIf you disagree with the answer, or it’s late, the ombudsman can look at it. You normally have six months from the final response.

What a catalogue refund looks like

If a complaint is upheld, the provider usually removes interest and charges on the part of the balance above the last fair limit, from the date of the unfair increase.

If you still owe money, the refund reduces the balance first. Anything left over is paid to you with interest added, and missed payment markers from that lending can be removed.

Questions

Can I claim back catalogue interest online?

Yes. You can complain to the catalogue provider online, or start with our check, which takes a few minutes.

Is a catalogue account the same as a credit card?

For lending purposes, yes. It’s a running account with a limit and interest, so the same affordability rules apply.

I have accounts with several brands. Do I complain to each?

Check who provides the credit. Several brands can belong to one provider, and one complaint can cover all of them.

Can I claim for a closed catalogue account?

Yes. Closed accounts can still be looked at, subject to the time limits.

How far back can I claim catalogue interest?

Usually six years from the lending, or three years from when you realised you had cause to complain, whichever is later.
