Logbook loan refund claims

The short answer

If a logbook lender didn’t properly check you could afford the repayments, you could get back everything you paid above the amount you borrowed, with interest. Upheld complaints have also seen the bill of sale cancelled.

What counts as unaffordable logbook loan lending

A logbook loan is borrowing secured against a vehicle you already own. You sign a credit agreement and a bill of sale, which means the lender owns the vehicle until the loan is repaid. You keep driving it, but if you don’t repay, the lender can take it and sell it.

Logbook loans are expensive, with APRs that can run into the hundreds. Because they are secured, they fall outside the price cap on payday loans. The lender still had to check you could afford the repayments, in the same way as any other lender.

  • Apr 2014Under the FCA’s rules, logbook lenders must check you can afford the loan, in proportion to how much you borrow and your circumstances.
  • Nov 2018More detailed affordability rules, covering income, essential outgoings and other debts.
  • ArrearsThe ombudsman expects the lender to work with you if you fall behind, and to talk to you before taking and selling the vehicle.

Signs you could have a claim

  1. 01The weekly or monthly payments were too muchIf paying the loan left too little for rent, bills and food, it wasn’t affordable.
  2. 02You had other debts or defaultsCounty Court Judgments, defaults or undeclared debts were things proper checks would have found.
  3. 03Your income was irregularVariable income means the lender should have looked harder at whether you could keep up.
  4. 04You refinanced or took a second loanTopping up a logbook loan before the first was repaid can show the borrowing had become a problem.
  5. 05Your bank statements showed warning signsGambling, payday loans or returned payments were there to be seen if the lender looked.

If a complaint is upheld, you usually only repay what you borrowed. Anything you paid above that comes back to you, with interest added.

In upheld decisions the lender has also been told to cancel the bill of sale, so it no longer has a claim on your vehicle, and to pay for distress caused where a car was taken.

Ombudsman decisions on logbook loan complaints

Published Financial Ombudsman decisions show how these complaints are judged. Every complaint is decided on its own facts, so these are examples, not a guide to what you’ll get.

  • UpheldLogbook Money

    Bank statements showed undeclared debts, existing defaults and gambling, which should have led to more checks. The lender had to refund payments above the £750 borrowed, remove negative credit information and cancel the bill of sale.

    Read decision DRN-3027217

  • UpheldLogbook Money

    A £1,000 loan repayable as £2,800 over 78 weeks. The checks weren’t proportionate and the loan was probably unaffordable. The lender wrote off the balance, refunded payments above the amount borrowed and paid £200 for the distress of the car being taken.

    Read decision DRN-3554979

  • UpheldLoans 2 Go

    The lender ignored warning signs across several logbook loans, including an undeclared County Court Judgment, repeated refinancing and an attachment of earnings order.

    Read decision DRN-2725790

Logbook loan claim questions

Can I claim if the lender took my car?

Yes. You can complain about the lending decision and about how the lender handled the arrears and repossession.

Can I claim if I’ve paid it off?

Yes. Repaid loans can still be looked at, subject to the time limits.

Is a logbook loan the same as car finance?

No. With car finance the lender pays for the car. With a logbook loan you already own the car and borrow against it. Both can be complained about if they weren’t affordable.

Why wasn’t my logbook loan covered by the payday price cap?

The cap only applies to unsecured high-cost short-term credit. A logbook loan is secured on your vehicle, so it falls outside it.

How far back can I claim?

Usually six years from the loan, or three years from when you realised you had cause to complain, whichever is later.

How to complain about a logbook loan yourself

You can complain to the lender directly and for free. Give the loan details and say you want the lender’s affordability checks reviewed.

The lender has eight weeks to reply. If you’re unhappy with the answer, or it hasn’t replied in time, you can take it to the Financial Ombudsman, normally within six months of the final response. That’s free too.
