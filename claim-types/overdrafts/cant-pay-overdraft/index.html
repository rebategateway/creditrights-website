Can’t pay back your overdraft?

The short answer

An overdraft has no fixed repayment date, but your bank can ask for it back. If you can’t clear it, tell your bank: it has to treat you fairly and consider options such as reducing or waiving interest and charges. If you were stuck in your overdraft for months and the bank didn’t step in, you could also claim back the interest and charges.

How an overdraft is paid back

There are no monthly instalments. Money you pay into the account reduces what you owe, and interest is charged on the balance while you’re overdrawn. Since April 2020 each overdraft has had a single interest rate and no fixed fees.

Overdrafts are usually repayable on demand. Santander’s terms, for example, say it can ask for the full amount back at any time, and that it normally gives 30 days’ notice before reducing or removing a limit. Your own bank’s overdraft terms set out what it does.

What happens if you don’t pay your overdraft

If you stay over your limit, or don’t repay when the bank asks, things usually escalate step by step:

  1. 01The bank contacts youIt writes, texts or calls about the balance and may reduce your limit or stop further borrowing.
  2. 02The account can be defaultedA default stays on your credit file for six years from the date of default, even if you pay it off.
  3. 03The debt can be passed onThe bank may pass or sell the debt to a collection agency, which then asks you to pay.
  4. 04Court action is possibleAs with any unpaid debt, the bank or the agency can go to court for the money.

What your bank has to do

FCA rules require banks to spot customers who use their overdraft repeatedly and those showing signs of financial difficulty. The bank has to tell you about the pattern, encourage you to get in touch, give you details of free debt advice and, if it carries on, set out options to reduce your use.

Those options can include reducing or waiving interest and charges. A bank shouldn’t cut or remove a repeat user’s overdraft if that would cause them financial hardship.

You could claim back the interest and charges

If you were overdrawn most of every month, your limit went up while you were struggling, or the bank never got in touch, the interest and charges from the point it should have stepped in can be refunded.

You can claim while you still owe the overdraft. If the claim succeeds, the refund normally comes off your balance first, which can clear part or all of what you owe.

Questions

What happens if I can’t pay my overdraft?

Tell your bank. It has to consider options such as reducing or waiving interest and charges. If the balance isn’t repaid, the account can be defaulted and the debt passed to a collection agency.

When do you have to pay back an overdraft?

There’s no fixed date, but overdrafts are usually repayable on demand. Banks normally give notice before reducing or removing a limit.

Can my bank take my overdraft away?

Yes. If you’re a repeat user, it shouldn’t remove or cut the overdraft in a way that causes you financial hardship.

How long does an overdraft default stay on my credit file?

Six years from the date of default, even if you pay the debt off.

Can I claim if I still owe money on my overdraft?

Yes. If the claim succeeds, the refund normally reduces your balance first.
