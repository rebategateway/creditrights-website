How to claim back overdraft charges

The short answer

You can claim back overdraft interest and charges if your bank should have seen the borrowing had become a problem: you were overdrawn most of every month, your limit went up while you were struggling, or charges kept building and nobody offered help. Finding the charges high isn’t enough on its own; the Supreme Court settled that in 2009.

When you can get overdraft charges back

An overdraft is credit, so your bank has to lend responsibly. Since December 2019 banks have also had to watch how each customer uses their overdraft, spot repeat use and step in when someone shows signs of financial difficulty.

Refunds come from that duty. If the bank kept charging you when it should have stepped in, or raised your limit when it shouldn’t have, the interest and charges from that point can be refunded. These are the patterns that most often lead to a refund:

  1. 01You were in your overdraft most of every monthYour balance only went above zero for a day or two after payday, month after month.
  2. 02Your limit went up while you were already using it allEach increase was a new lending decision the bank had to get right.
  3. 03Charges kept coming and nobody got in touchA growing balance, more days overdrawn and refused payments are signs the bank should act on.
  4. 04Your statements showed you were strugglingPayday loans, gambling or returned direct debits were all visible to the bank.

Unarranged overdraft fees and the 2009 ruling

In the late 2000s thousands of people tried to reclaim unarranged overdraft and returned payment fees as unfair. On 25 November 2009 the Supreme Court ruled that those charges were part of the price of a current account, so their fairness couldn’t be challenged that way. Claims made only on the grounds that the fees were too high stopped there.

The rules have changed since. From April 2020 banks could no longer charge fixed daily or monthly overdraft fees, and an unarranged overdraft can cost no more than an arranged one. Claims today are about something the 2009 ruling didn’t cover: whether your bank lent responsibly and stepped in when it should have.

How to get overdraft charges refunded

There are two ways to claim. You can complain to your bank yourself, or use our check and we introduce your claim to a specialist who makes the complaint for you.

  1. 01Work out when the problem startedRoughly when you started living in your overdraft, or when the limit went up. You don’t need statements: the bank holds them.
  2. 02Complain to your bankSay you relied on the overdraft, from when, and that you want the interest and charges refunded from the point the bank should have stepped in.
  3. 03Wait up to eight weeksThe bank has eight weeks to send its final response.
  4. 04Take it to the Financial OmbudsmanIf you disagree with the answer, or it’s late, the ombudsman can look at it. You normally have six months from the final response.

What an overdraft refund looks like

If your complaint is upheld, the bank usually refunds the interest and charges from the date it should have stepped in or shouldn’t have raised the limit, and adds interest to money paid back to you.

If you’re still overdrawn, the refund normally comes off your balance first. Negative marks the bank added to your credit file because of that borrowing can be removed.

If you’re struggling with your overdraft now

Tell your bank. When a customer is in financial difficulty, the FCA expects banks to consider options such as reducing or waiving interest and charges. Our guide to what happens if you can’t pay back your overdraft explains what your bank has to do.

Questions

What is an overdraft refund?

A refund of overdraft interest and charges your bank shouldn’t have taken, usually because it kept lending when it should have seen the overdraft had become a problem.

Can I get unarranged overdraft fees back?

Not just because they were high: the Supreme Court ruled in 2009 that their fairness couldn’t be challenged that way. They can be refunded as part of a claim that your bank should have stepped in.

Can I ask my bank to reverse overdraft fees?

Yes, you can ask at any time. If you’re in financial difficulty, the FCA expects banks to consider reducing or waiving interest and charges.

How far back can I claim overdraft charges?

Usually six years from the lending, or three years from when you realised you had cause to complain, whichever is later.

Can I claim for an account I’ve closed?

Yes. Closed accounts can still be looked at, subject to the same time limits.

Do I need my bank statements?

No. The bank holds your statements and looks at them when it reviews the complaint.
